## 1 Start here

### 1.1 Signing in to your work or school account

- `1.1.1` Sign in to your work or school account with your work email address.
- `1.1.2` Sign out of a computer other people use.
- `1.1.3` Refuse to save a password on a computer others use.

### 1.2 What two-step sign-in is, and why

- `1.2.1` Explain two-step sign-in as a password plus something you have.
- `1.2.2` Explain why a stolen password alone no longer opens the account.
- `1.2.3` Refuse to share a sign-in code with anyone.

### 1.3 Setting up two-step sign-in

- `1.3.1` Install the authenticator app your account uses.
- `1.3.2` Deny a sign-in request you did not start, and report it.
- `1.3.3` Move two-step sign-in to a new phone before resetting the old one.

### 1.4 A strong password

- `1.4.1` Make a password long: four or more ordinary words.
- `1.4.2` Use a different password for work than for anything personal.
- `1.4.3` Never give your password to anyone, IT included.

### 1.5 Spotting a scam email

- `1.5.1` Recognise a scam email by its sender, its urgency and its request.
- `1.5.2` Check where a link goes before clicking it.
- `1.5.3` Report a click on a scam link straight away.

### 1.6 Scams on WhatsApp, by text and by phone

- `1.6.1` Never forward a code that arrives by text or WhatsApp.
- `1.6.2` Check an urgent request from a senior colleague by calling them on a number you already have.
- `1.6.3` Turn on WhatsApp's own two-step verification.

### 1.7 Where your files go

- `1.7.1` Save work files in your work cloud storage.
- `1.7.2` Explain why a file in cloud storage survives a lost laptop.
- `1.7.3` Read the cloud status of a file in File Explorer.

### 1.8 Who to call

- `1.8.1` Name the person who looks after IT, and how to reach them.
- `1.8.2` Report a clicked scam link or a lost device straight away.
- `1.8.3` Report your own mistake without waiting.

## 2 Your computer and phone

### 2.1 The Start menu, taskbar and search

- `2.1.1` Open any app, file or setting by searching from the Start menu.
- `2.1.2` Shut down or restart from the Start menu.
- `2.1.3` Pin the app you use most to the taskbar.

### 2.2 Files and folders in File Explorer

- `2.2.1` Browse files and folders in File Explorer.
- `2.2.2` Make a new folder.
- `2.2.3` Move a file into a folder.

### 2.3 Naming and organising files

- `2.3.1` Name a file so it can be found again.
- `2.3.2` Put the date first, as year-month-day, so files sort in order.
- `2.3.3` Keep a few clear folders you actually use.

### 2.4 Where downloads go

- `2.4.1` Find the files you downloaded.
- `2.4.2` Move a download you need into the right folder.
- `2.4.3` Delete, unopened, a download you did not ask for.

### 2.5 The browser: tabs and bookmarks

- `2.5.1` Open and close tabs with the keyboard.
- `2.5.2` Bring back a tab closed by mistake.
- `2.5.3` Bookmark a page you use often.

### 2.6 Searching well

- `2.6.1` Search for an exact phrase with quotes.
- `2.6.2` Search one website with site:.
- `2.6.3` Check who wrote a result before trusting it.

### 2.7 Printing

- `2.7.1` Open printing from any app with Ctrl + P.
- `2.7.2` Print only the pages you need.
- `2.7.3` Check the printer's name before printing.

### 2.8 Scanning with your phone

- `2.8.1` Scan a document with your phone into a PDF.
- `2.8.2` Save a scan of a record to work storage, not your photo gallery.
- `2.8.3` Take a clean scan: a plain surface in good light.

### 2.9 Keyboard shortcuts that save time

- `2.9.1` Copy, cut and paste with the keyboard.
- `2.9.2` Undo a mistake with Ctrl + Z.
- `2.9.3` Lock the computer when you step away.

### 2.10 When the Wi-Fi fails

- `2.10.1` Tell a network problem from a problem on your own computer.
- `2.10.2` Check airplane mode and Wi-Fi before anything else.
- `2.10.3` Tell IT what happened and what you already tried.

## 3 Communicating

### 3.1 Writing a clear work email

- `3.1.1` Write a subject line that says what you need.
- `3.1.2` Put the main point in the first sentence.
- `3.1.3` Keep one topic per email.

### 3.2 Sending, replying, forwarding and attachments

- `3.2.1` Choose between Reply and Reply all.
- `3.2.2` Forward an email to someone who was not on it.
- `3.2.3` Check an attachment is there before sending.

### 3.3 To, Cc and Bcc

- `3.3.1` Put the people who must act in To.
- `3.3.2` Put people who only need to know in Cc.
- `3.3.3` Hide recipients' addresses from each other with Bcc.

### 3.4 Keeping your inbox in order

- `3.4.1` Organise email into a few folders or labels.
- `3.4.2` Keep only as many folders as you use.
- `3.4.3` Flag or star an email you must act on later.

### 3.5 Finding an old email

- `3.5.1` Search email by sender.
- `3.5.2` Narrow a search to emails with attachments.
- `3.5.3` Search every folder, not only the inbox.

### 3.6 WhatsApp at work

- `3.6.1` Reply to one person directly when only they need it.
- `3.6.2` Keep students' marks and personal details out of groups that do not need them.
- `3.6.3` Mute a busy group rather than leaving it.

### 3.7 WhatsApp on your computer

- `3.7.1` Link WhatsApp to a computer.
- `3.7.2` Log out of WhatsApp on a shared computer.
- `3.7.3` Log a computer out of WhatsApp from your phone.

### 3.8 Scheduling a meeting

- `3.8.1` Write an invite with a title, time, place or link, and purpose.
- `3.8.2` Use a calendar invite rather than a message for a meeting.
- `3.8.3` Check when people are free before inviting them.

### 3.9 Choosing the channel

- `3.9.1` Use email for what must be kept on record or goes outside.
- `3.9.2` Use WhatsApp for quick coordination with colleagues.
- `3.9.3` Phone when something is urgent or sensitive.
