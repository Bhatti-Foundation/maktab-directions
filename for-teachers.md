# Directions for teachers

We are creating a world-class experience for BIC's students, in the classroom and online. For your teaching to reach them in Maktab, your content needs to be in one consistent shape, and your AI can do most of that for you ([the directions it follows](README.md)). This page explains the shape and why, so you can write in it from the start and check what your AI gives back.

## Maktab is a website, not a handout

Your students read Maktab on a phone, one short part at a time, with the class's book beside them. That changes what a lesson needs:

| On paper | On Maktab |
|---|---|
| Answers at the back of the booklet | Students see each answer after they try the question |
| Spaces and dotted lines to write answers in | Students type their answers into Maktab, so answer spaces are left out |
| "See the coursebook, p. 46" | The class's book opens beside each sub-topic at the right page, so book references are left out |
| A cover, *How to use this booklet*, a colour key | Maktab has its own pages for these, so they are left out |
| A drawing made of typed characters, a handwritten note | A picture, and typed words. Handwriting never reaches a student |
| A long section, split into pages | Short parts of one idea each, read one screen after another. Your AI splits a part over about 1,500 words (around 19 phone screens) |
| Questions you check by hand | Questions Maktab asks, checks and marks, so each one needs its answer |

Your lesson adds to the book: your explanations, your examples, the mistakes you see, what earns marks. Where it would only repeat the book, leave it out.

Your own file stays as it is. Your AI gives you a copy for Maktab. Anything true only on paper (*Take a break*, *Learner's name: ____*) goes in a box whose first line is `Print only`, and Maktab leaves that box out.

## Headings

- **Each sub-topic is a Heading 1 with its exact Cambridge number and title**, for example `3.1 Momentum and Newton's laws of motion`. Use Word's styles (Home › Styles), not bold text, a shaded band or a table.
- **Your own section titles are Heading 2s, with no number**, for example `Mass and inertia`. Maktab reads a number at the start of a heading as a Cambridge sub-topic.
- **Each title says in 3 to 8 words what the part teaches.** *Testing for reducing sugars* works; *Part A* does not.
- **Optional extras:**
  - A sub-topic can open with a Heading 2 **Start here** (what to know first) and close with a Heading 2 **Wrap up** (your summary). Students see these as the sub-topic's first and last screens.
  - The whole topic can open with a Heading 1 **Introduction** and close with a Heading 1 **Summary**, for your glossary and chapter summary.

## Pictures, tables, lists, links and formulas

- **Pictures:** paste each picture (PNG or JPEG) on a line of its own. A chart, or a drawing made of Word's shapes, doesn't come in, so paste a picture of it.
- **Captions:** put the caption on the line just under the picture, starting with its name and number: `Figure 2.1 A mass spectrum`. It must start that way to be read as the caption; `Figure: A mass spectrum` comes in as an ordinary paragraph. For a table, put its title on the line above it: `Table 2.1 Reacting masses`.
- **Attribution, if you give one:** to say where a figure, table or picture came from, add it to the end of its caption: `Figure 2.1 A mass spectrum. Source: Chemistry Coursebook, Cambridge University Press`. Your AI keeps any attribution your file already has, and moves it there for you.
- **Tables:** a table of data stays a table, and a picture in a table cell comes in where it stands.
- **Lists:** use Word's bullets and numbering (Home › Bullets), up to three levels.
- **Links:** a link inside a sentence comes in as a link. A YouTube or Vimeo link on a line of its own comes in as a video, playing where it stands. A picture is pasted into the file, not linked to.
- **Formulas:** type them as ordinary text, using Word's superscripts and subscripts: `Eₖ = ½mv²`, `m s⁻²`. Maktab can't read a formula made with Word's equation tool (Insert › Equation): the words around it come in, but the formula doesn't. Your AI can retype one for you.
- **Bold and italic** come in as you set them.

## Boxes

Textbooks use boxes to draw the eye and break up long text: a definition to learn, a tip, a common mistake. Maktab does the same, and each type of box tells the student what to do with it.

**To make one, use a Word table with one row and one column.** The first line says what type of box it is, alone or followed by a short title: `Definition: Momentum`. The rest of the table is what the box holds: words, lists, pictures and formulas. Put any table or video after the box, not inside it.

> **Definition: Momentum**
> The momentum of a body is the product of its mass and its velocity.

There are eight types of boxes:

| Box | What the student does |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Learn these words exactly |
| **Key idea** | Remember this. The rest builds on it |
| **Derivation** | See where this result comes from |
| **Tip** | Use this in your answers. Your exam tips, shortcuts and memory tricks |
| **Common mistake** | Many students get this wrong. Check you don't |
| **Link** | See where this idea is used: in the real world, in practical work, later in the course. Its title says which |
| **Think about it** | Stop and think before you read on |
| **Teacher's note** | For teachers only. Students never see it |

- **Your own names are read too:** *Exam tip*, *Examiner's advice*, *Top tip* and *Memory trick* are all a **Tip**; *Real-world link*, *Practical skills link* and *Syllabus link* are all a **Link**.
- **A box that fits none of these** starts with `Box` and your title, and stays a plain box.
- **One table makes one box.** Start a new table for the next box, and start your next paragraph after the table.

## Worked examples

A worked example is your teaching: you solve a problem in front of the student. It stays in your lesson, where you put it. Start a one-by-one table with `Worked example: A rocket in flight`, then the problem, then one step to a line, then the answer:

> **Worked example: A rocket in flight**
> A rocket of mass 2000 kg …
> 1. …
> 2. …
> Answer: …

## Quick checks

The **Quick check** tab in each sub-topic has short questions that make sure students have picked up the idea they've just read: one idea at a time, tried until they get it right.

Where you don't give quick check questions, Maktab writes its own, but it's happy to take yours. **To add yours, use a *Check your understanding* box:** a one-by-one table whose first line is `Check your understanding`, then the question, then its answer. Put one at the end of each section.

> **Check your understanding**
> A 0.50 kg ball moves at 8.0 m s⁻¹. What is its momentum?
> Answer: 4.0 kg m s⁻¹

- **`Answer:` is the final value alone.** Your working, if you give it, goes after the box: a line `SOLUTION`, then one numbered step to a line. Steps written after `Answer:` are taken as the answer, and students are marked against all of them. For a question in parts, put each part's name, `(a)(i)`, on a line of its own above that part's steps, so each part's steps show under that part.
- **Each question stands alone.** Quick check is another tab from your lesson, so nothing is *above* or *below* it there. Instead of *In the worked example above…*, give the question the situation and values it needs. Your AI copies them in for you where you have not.

## Practice questions

The **Practise** tab has whole problems that students work through with help. **Any question with your step-by-step working is a practice question:** a past paper question with your solution under it, or a question of your own. At any step, a student who needs help presses **Hint** and sees your solution, one step at a time. So write one step to a line.

For a question of your own, use a one-by-one table whose first line is `Question`:

> **Question**
> A trolley of mass 1.5 kg moves at 2.0 m s⁻¹ and hits a wall …
> 1. …
> 2. …
> Answer: …

For a multiple-choice question of your own, type each option on its own line with its letter (`A`, `B`, `C`, `D`), and give the answer: `Answer: B`.

## Past paper questions

The **Past paper questions** tab has Cambridge's questions, which students answer on their own, as Cambridge asks them, marked against the mark scheme.

**The reference alone is enough.** A line such as `9618/12/M/J/21 Q5` brings in the question, its pictures, its options if it is multiple choice, its marks and Cambridge's answer. You don't type any of them.

That needs Cambridge's question number. Where you have only the paper, write it and type the question under it: `9618/12/M/J/21`, then the question. Maktab finds it on the paper by its words.

Add your step-by-step solution under it, a line `SOLUTION` then one step to a line, and the question also goes to Practise, where students can work through it with hints. After they answer on Past paper questions, it is the solution they compare with their own.

## Question codes

- **A past paper reference** is written out in full: the subject and paper, the series (`M/J`, `O/N`, `F/M`), the year in two figures, then the question number. For example `9701/11/M/J/15 Q5`, not `M/J-15-11`.
- **Use Cambridge's question number, never your own.** If your worksheet calls it *Question 2* but it was question 5 on Cambridge's paper, write `Q5`. A wrong number brings in a different question, with the wrong answer and mark scheme.
- **If you don't know Cambridge's number, leave it off**, write only the paper, `9701/11/M/J/15`, and type the question under it.

## How you give answers

In your file, put each answer straight under its question, never in a section at the back.

| Question | What you write |
|---|---|
| Quick check | `Answer:` and the final value alone, on the last line of the box; any working after the box, under `SOLUTION` |
| Practice question | Your working, one step to a line, then `Answer:` |
| Worked example | Your working, one step to a line, then `Answer:` |
| Past paper, multiple choice | Nothing: Maktab uses Cambridge's key. To add your reason, write `Answer: B (ionic bonds need two elements)` |
| Past paper, structured | Nothing: Maktab uses Cambridge's mark scheme. To use your own, write `Mark scheme:` under the question, keeping each point's code (`M1`, `A1`) on its line |

**Your working is worth giving.** In Practise it is the hint students reveal a step at a time, and after they answer it is the solution they compare with their own.

Your answer always wins over Cambridge's. If your file has an answer sheet at the back, your AI moves each answer under its question.

> ### ⚠ A question with no answer is never asked
> Where you gave no answer, your AI suggests one. **Students don't see that question until you confirm the answer.** To confirm it, open the sub-topic and press **Edit**. The suggested answer is in the question's answer box, marked **Suggested by AI**. Press **Looks right** under *Please check* to keep it, or type your own. Maktab also lists these questions when you publish.

## Learning outcomes

You don't write learning outcomes. On a sub-topic, press **Edit**, then **Clean up with AI**: it tags each part of your lesson and each of your questions with the learning outcomes it teaches. You can change any of them in the editor.

## Importing your files

On **Class materials**, press **Export or import** and choose your files. You can bring in several Word files at once, or a zip of them. Maktab shows what each file changes, side by side with your file, before anything is added.

**Organise your files however suits you.** Each file brings its pieces to the sub-topics its headings name, so one sub-topic can be fed by several files: your notes on 3.1 in one, your past paper questions on 3.1 in another.

**A file's name is how Maktab knows it:**
- **A new version of the same file keeps the same name.** Importing it replaces what the last version brought in. A past paper question you take out of your file stays on the page: delete it there.
- **Different material gets a different name.** It's added alongside. So if the same notes are imported under two names, students read them twice.

**If you don't like how something came in,** change your file, save it, and press **Read again** on the import screen. Maktab reads the file again before anything is added.

**All changes come in unpublished.** You can edit them, undo them, or press **Preview as a learner** to see them as students will, before you publish. Nothing reaches a student until you publish it.

## Your file and the editor

**Your Word file is the master copy of your teaching.** Make your changes there and import it again, under the same name.

**The editor** (press **Edit** on a sub-topic) is for quick changes on the page: choosing a box's type, answering each *Please check*, confirming a suggested answer, choosing videos, or correcting a word you spot while reading as a student.

**Maktab never overwrites your changes on the page.** For example, you fix a typo in 3.1 on the page. A week later you import a new version of your file, which still has the typo. Maktab keeps your fix and tells you the file says something different. Anything you added only on the page, such as a video, also stays. To keep your file and the page the same, make each fix in your file too.

## Lesson videos

- **In your file:** put the video's YouTube link on a line of its own, where it belongs in your lesson.
- **On the page:**
  1. Open the sub-topic and press **Edit**.
  2. The main video sits at the top of **Learn**. Click it, paste your video's link under **Link**, and press **Save**.
  3. To add another video, press **＋** where it belongs in your lesson and paste its link there.

The video plays before you save, with its title from YouTube.
